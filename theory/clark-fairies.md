## Paraphrased points
- Evans-Wentz (Oxford PhD): toured Celtic British Isles + Brittany first decade 1900s → The Fairy Faith in Celtic Countries (1911, PUBLIC DOMAIN — quotable). Substrate: Rev. Robert Kirk, The Secret Common-Wealth (1691, PUBLIC DOMAIN). Thesis: fairies occupy an overlapping otherworld.
- Witness class crosses lines: 17th-c. Swedish clergyman Peter Rahm gave sworn legal deposition — not only rural folk.
- 1907 capture (Lady Campbell informants, Irish glen, poverty): 2ft leprechaun, dark clear skin, red hair, red cap, green clothes, boots. Carried home, locked in, fed bread + rice + milk from spoon-cup. Planned city exhibition. Cage left open nights; escaped. Family fortunes collapse after; sight lost.
- Counter-case (Gregory/Yeats via the Kellehers, Wicklow Mountains): domesticated fairy kept well — treatment decides outcome.
- Cottingley 1917-20: Frances Griffiths (10) + Elsie Wright (13), borrowed camera, single plate → four tiny winged women before Frances (paper-cutout look). Second plate: gnome Elsie invites to her lap. Father confiscates camera. Truth emerges ~six decades later.
